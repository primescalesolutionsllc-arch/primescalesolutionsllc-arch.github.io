In-house callers vs a managed team: what it really costs

Hiring a caller looks cheap on paper. Here is what usually gets left out of the math.

The hourly rate is the smallest part of the cost of a cold caller. The real cost is everything around it.

Recruiting. Posting jobs, screening, interviews and voice tests. Then doing it again when someone quits in month two.

Training and management. Scripts, market knowledge, daily call reviews and coaching. If nobody does this, performance slowly drops and you do not notice until the pipeline is empty.

Tools and data. Dialer seats, phone numbers, number health, lists and skip tracing all add up, and someone has to manage them.

Downtime. Every no-show, every resignation and every flagged number is lost dialing time.

A managed team rolls all of this into one monthly price, with a manager who answers for the numbers. For most investors, the question is not which is cheaper per hour, but which one produces more deals per dollar.
